# Website Redesign Checklist for 2026: 15 Things to Review Before Launch

**Published:** September 2026  
**Reading time:** 12 minutes  
**Target audience:** Business owners, marketing directors, decision-makers  
**SEO focus:** website redesign, website best practices, web design checklist

---

## Before You Hire a Designer or Launch a Redesign

A website redesign is expensive (typically $15K–$100K). A bad redesign is expensive *twice over* because you lose traffic, leads, and credibility.

This checklist prevents that.

Use it before you start design work. Use it again before you launch. Use it 30 days after launch to catch issues early.

---

## Phase 1: Strategy (Before Design)

### 1. Define Your Conversion Goals

❓ **What's the one thing you want visitors to do?**

Not five things. One.

- Book a consultation? ✓
- Sign up for a newsletter? ✓
- Download a guide? ✓
- Make a purchase? ✓

Everything else on your site should support this goal.

**Red flag:** "We want leads AND sales AND brand awareness AND newsletter signups." Pick one primary goal. Add one secondary goal max.

**Action:**
- [ ] Write your primary conversion goal in one sentence.
- [ ] Map every page to that goal (how does it support it?).
- [ ] Remove or repurpose pages that don't support it.

---

### 2. Understand Your Visitors (Personas, Not Assumptions)

❓ **Who are you actually trying to reach?**

Don't guess. Use data.

**Pull from:**
- Google Analytics: Who visits? Where do they come from? What do they do?
- HubSpot/CRM: What info do successful customers have in common?
- Interviews: Call 5 customers who bought in the last 6 months. Ask: "Why us? What were you looking for?"
- Surveys: Send a 2-minute survey to recent visitors and customers.

**For each audience segment:**
- [ ] Age, job title, industry
- [ ] Pain points (what problem are they solving?)
- [ ] How they find you (organic search, referral, ads?)
- [ ] Decision-making process (how long? who's involved? what matters?)
- [ ] What content/proof matters most (reviews, case studies, pricing transparency?)

**Example:**
```
Persona: Sarah (Operations Manager, B2B SaaS)
- Age 32, oversees 5-person ops team
- Pain: 20 hours/week lost to manual data entry
- How she finds us: Google search "workflow automation"
- Needs: Proof it works, case study from similar company, pricing transparency
- Timeline: Looking to solve by end of Q1
```

---

### 3. Audit Your Current Site (Be Honest)

❓ **What's working? What's broken? What's wasting visitors' time?**

Use Google Analytics 4 to answer:

- [ ] **Traffic sources:** Where do visitors come from? (organic, paid, direct, referral?)
- [ ] **Top landing pages:** Which pages get the most visits?
- [ ] **Bounce rate:** Which pages do people leave immediately? (>70% is bad)
- [ ] **Conversion rate:** Which pages drive the most leads/sales?
- [ ] **Time on page:** Are people actually reading, or just bouncing?
- [ ] **Click paths:** What's the path from landing page to conversion? Is it too many clicks?

**Also test manually:**
- Can you find what you need in 3 clicks?
- Does the site feel slow? (Test at PageSpeed Insights)
- Do CTAs stand out or get lost?
- Are there technical errors? (404 links, broken forms, mobile issues?)

**Red flags to fix in a redesign:**
- Bounce rate over 70%
- Conversion rate under 1% (when it should be 2–3%)
- Average time on page under 1 minute
- Mobile traffic over 60% but mobile conversion rate is half of desktop

---

### 4. Competitive Landscape: What Are Competitors Doing Right?

❓ **Why would a visitor choose you over a competitor?**

Audit 3–5 main competitors:

- [ ] Homepage clarity: Can you understand their value in 5 seconds?
- [ ] CTA strategy: How many CTAs? Where? What action do they want?
- [ ] Content: Blog, case studies, pricing pages—what's there?
- [ ] Design: Mobile-friendly? Loading speed? Visual hierarchy?
- [ ] Trust signals: Reviews, logos, credentials, team bios?

**The goal:** Find the gap. Where are competitors weak? That's where you win.

**Example:**
```
Competitor A: Great design, but pricing is hidden (makes them seem expensive)
Competitor B: Lists pricing, but no customer reviews
Competitor C: Case studies, but site is slow (traffic lost to speed issues)

Opportunity: You put pricing up front, add 10 customer reviews, 
and make it faster than all of them.
```

## Phase 2: Design & Content (During Redesign)

### 5. Information Architecture: Structure Your Content Clearly

❓ **Can visitors find what they need in 3 clicks?**

Your site structure should match how visitors think, not how you think.

**Typical structure for a service business:**

```
Home
├── What We Do (Services)
│   ├── Service A (with subpages)
│   ├── Service B
│   └── Service C
├── Why Us (About + Proof)
│   ├── About / Team
│   ├── Case Studies
│   └── Reviews/Testimonials
├── Pricing & How It Works
│   ├── Service A Pricing
│   ├── Service B Pricing
│   └── Process
├── Blog / Resources
│   ├── Most Recent
│   ├── By Category
│   └── Search
├── Contact / Book a Call
└── FAQ
```

**Action:**
- [ ] Map your current site structure.
- [ ] Does it match how customers search for you?
- [ ] Can a visitor answer "How do I buy?" in 2 clicks?
- [ ] Can a visitor find pricing in under 10 seconds?

---

### 6. Homepage: Nail the First 5 Seconds

Your homepage has 5 seconds to answer three questions:

1. **What does this company do?** (Clear value proposition)
2. **Is this for me?** (Speaks to my specific problem)
3. **What do I do next?** (One clear CTA)

**Checklist for homepage:**
- [ ] Headline answers "What problem do you solve?" Not "Welcome to our company."
- [ ] Subheading clarifies who it's for. (Not "We help businesses." But "We help SaaS companies save 20+ hours/week on operations.")
- [ ] Hero image/video shows the solution in action (not just a pretty photo).
- [ ] Above the fold includes headline, subheading, primary CTA. No scrolling needed to see it.
- [ ] Primary CTA is clear, specific, and high-contrast. ("Get a Free Audit" > "Learn More")

**Example (Bad):**
```
Welcome to Acme Solutions
Your partner in digital transformation

[Vague hero image of people in a meeting]
[Three tiny CTAs: "Services" "About" "Contact"]
```

**Example (Good):**
```
Save 20+ Hours Per Week With AI Automation
For growing service businesses tired of manual data entry

[Image showing actual process: email → database → team notification]
[One big CTA: "Book a Free 30-Minute Strategy Call"]
```

---

### 7. Service Pages: Answer the Real Questions

Visitors land on your service pages asking:
- What is this?
- Why should I care?
- Will this solve my problem?
- How much does it cost?
- What happens next?

**Service page checklist:**
- [ ] Opening section defines the service in plain language (1–2 sentences).
- [ ] Second section: The problem it solves. ("Tired of spending 15 hours/week on...")
- [ ] Third section: How it works. (3–5 clear steps, not 10.)
- [ ] Case study or result. ("Client saw 40% increase in lead response time")
- [ ] Pricing is transparent. (Even "starts at $X" is better than hidden pricing.)
- [ ] Next step is clear. ("Schedule a consultation" with CTA.)
- [ ] FAQ section answers common questions.

**Red flag:** If your service page doesn't mention price, you're scaring away budget-conscious prospects and wasting designer time convincing them.

---

### 8. About Page: Build Trust (Be Specific)

Most about pages are generic ("We're passionate about helping businesses").

Good about pages tell a story and build credibility.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Founder story: Where did the company come from? Why does it exist?
- [ ] Credentials: What makes you qualified? (Years of experience, certifications, past roles?)
- [ ] Social proof: Logos of clients, press mentions, or industry recognition.
- [ ] Team bios: 1–2 line bio for each team member + photo.
- [ ] Values: What do you actually believe? (Not corporate buzzwords—real values.)
- [ ] "What we've done:" Revenue helped generate, hours saved, problems solved (numbers matter).

---

### 9. Content Audit: Blog, Resources, FAQ

❓ **What content should live on your new site?**

Don't copy everything from the old site. That's how you end up with 200 pages of outdated junk.

**Action:**
- [ ] List every piece of content on your current site.
- [ ] For each one: Keep? Update? Delete? Archive?
- [ ] **Rule of thumb:** If it hasn't been updated in 18 months, kill it or fix it.
- [ ] Implement blog category structure (e.g., "AI Automation," "Web Development," "Digital Marketing").
- [ ] Create FAQ section based on real customer questions (check your support tickets, emails, calls).

**Content you should prioritize:**
1. SEO blog posts that rank and drive traffic
2. Case studies with real numbers
3. Pricing/comparison content
4. FAQ answering common questions
5. Process/methodology content (builds authority)

**Content you can kill:**
- Outdated news posts ("Visit us at the 2022 tech conference")
- Thin pages with no substance (3 paragraphs of intro text)
- Duplicate content (same topic covered 3 ways, no unique angle)
- Pages that don't support your conversion goal

---

### 10. Mobile-First Design Check

❓ **Does it work on a phone?**

Over 60% of traffic is mobile. Mobile users bounce in 2 seconds if:
- Font is too small
- Buttons are too close together (can't tap accurately)
- Page takes >3 seconds to load
- CTA is at the bottom of a long page

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Test on actual phone (not just browser simulator).
- [ ] Font size 16px minimum for body text.
- [ ] Buttons and links: 44x44px minimum tap target (iPhone standard).
- [ ] Forms: One field per screen on mobile (easier on eyes).
- [ ] No auto-playing video or audio (instant bounce).
- [ ] Primary CTA visible without scrolling on mobile.
- [ ] Load time under 3 seconds (test at PageSpeed Insights).

## Phase 3: Technical & Performance

### 11. Page Speed & Core Web Vitals

Google ranks slow sites lower. Visitors bounce if pages take >3 seconds.

**Test at PageSpeed Insights (free tool):**
- [ ] Desktop score 90+
- [ ] Mobile score 80+ (mobile is harder)
- [ ] Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) <2.5s
- [ ] Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) <0.1 (no random element jumps)
- [ ] Interaction to Next Paint (INP) <200ms

**Common fixes:**
- Image optimization (use WebP format, compress)
- Lazy loading (images load as you scroll, not all at once)
- Minify CSS/JavaScript (remove unnecessary code)
- Reduce third-party scripts (analytics tools, chat widgets slow sites down)
- Use a content delivery network (CDN) to serve images from servers near visitors

**Rule of thumb:** Optimize images first. That's usually 50%+ of a slow site.

---

### 12. SEO Foundations

Your new site should be *more* SEO-friendly than the old one.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Each page has a unique, descriptive title (50–60 characters).
- [ ] Meta descriptions are written for clicks (160 characters, includes benefit/action).
- [ ] H1 tag appears once per page (your main headline).
- [ ] H2, H3 tags structure content logically.
- [ ] Images have descriptive alt text.
- [ ] Internal links point to relevant pages (helps Google understand site structure).
- [ ] 301 redirects set up: Old URLs → New URLs (so old traffic doesn't disappear).
- [ ] Sitemap.xml and robots.txt configured.
- [ ] Google Search Console connected.
- [ ] Schema markup added (Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage—depends on industry).

**Common mistake:** Forgetting 301 redirects. Old pages get to 0 traffic overnight, and Google thinks you deleted quality content.

---

### 13. Security & Compliance

❓ **Is visitor data protected?**

- [ ] SSL certificate (HTTPS) installed. (Green lock icon in browser address bar.)
- [ ] GDPR compliance: Privacy policy addresses data collection and visitor rights.
- [ ] CCPA/CPRA if you serve California visitors.
- [ ] Terms of service if you collect user data.
- [ ] Backup plan documented (who backs up the site? How often?).
- [ ] Content Delivery Network (CDN) configured if you target international users.

---

### 14. Analytics & Tracking

❓ **How will you measure success after launch?**

Set up tracking before launch:

- [ ] Google Analytics 4 configured (tracks visitors, conversions, goals).
- [ ] Conversion goals defined in GA4 (form submission, email signup, phone call, etc.).
- [ ] Heatmap tool installed (shows where visitors click, scroll, leave).
- [ ] Form analytics set up (which form fields confuse visitors?).
- [ ] CRM connected (new site leads flow into your CRM automatically).

---

## Phase 4: Pre-Launch

### 15. Pre-Launch Testing Checklist

Before you flip the switch, test everything:

- [ ] **Forms:** Submit a form from desktop and mobile. Does it work? Does the reply reach your inbox?
- [ ] **Links:** Crawl the site with a tool (Screaming Frog) to find broken links.
- [ ] **Navigation:** Can you reach every page from the menu? No dead ends?
- [ ] **Mobile:** Test on iPhone and Android. Scroll, tap buttons, submit forms.
- [ ] **Email signup:** Subscribe to your newsletter. Does confirmation email arrive?
- [ ] **Search:** Use site search (if you have it). Does it find content?
- [ ] **Browser compatibility:** Test in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge.
- [ ] **Redirects:** Old site URL → New site URL. Does the redirect work? (Test 5–10 old URLs.)
- [ ] **Email deliverability:** Check that transactional emails (form submissions, confirmations) don't land in spam.
- [ ] **Load time under traffic:** Have 5 people visit simultaneously. Does the site stay fast?

## Post-Launch: 30-Day Checkup

### Monitor These Metrics
- [ ] Traffic: Is it stable or down? (If down, check 301 redirects first.)
- [ ] Conversion rate: Did it improve? (Compare to old site.)
- [ ] Bounce rate: Did it improve? (Measure by page.)
- [ ] Average time on page: Are people reading more?
- [ ] User feedback: Check support tickets, calls, emails. Any complaints?

### Common Post-Launch Issues
- **Reduced traffic:** Usually 301 redirects were missed. Check Search Console for 404 errors.
- **Lost conversions:** Did form submissions still work? Check your email inbox.
- **Slow site:** Forgot image optimization or script minification. Run PageSpeed again.
- **Mobile issues:** Small bugs on mobile. Test on actual phones, not browser simulator.

## The Redesign Timeline

| Phase | Duration | Cost |
|-------|----------|------|
| Strategy (steps 1–4) | 1–2 weeks | $0–1,000 (internal or consultant) |
| Design & Content (steps 5–10) | 3–6 weeks | $5,000–20,000 |
| Technical setup (steps 11–14) | 1–2 weeks | $500–2,000 |
| Testing (step 15) | 1 week | $0 |
| **Total** | **6–11 weeks** | **$6,000–23,000** |

**Pro tip:** Don't rush. A good redesign takes 2–3 months. A rushed redesign will need another redesign in 6 months.

## Red Flags That Suggest You Need a Professional

You should hire help if:
- ❌ You haven't touched the site in 2+ years.
- ❌ Bounce rate is over 70%.
- ❌ Conversion rate is under 0.5%.
- ❌ Mobile traffic is over 60% but mobile conversion is significantly lower.
- ❌ You're competing in a crowded industry (you need strategy, not just a pretty design).

## Quick Wins Before a Full Redesign

If you're not ready for a full redesign, start here:

1. **Update homepage CTA.** Make it specific and high-contrast.
2. **Add pricing to service pages.** Transparency builds trust.
3. **Optimize images.** Run PageSpeed, compress images. Instant speed gain.
4. **Add 3 customer testimonials** with photos to your site.
5. **Set up 301 redirects** if you ever moved URLs.

These 5 things take 1–2 weeks and can improve conversion 20–30%.
